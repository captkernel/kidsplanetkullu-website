# What earns a school trust in Kullu — and how the site should carry it

Depth brief behind the content direction. The point Karan made is right: in a valley
community, reputation is built offline (elders, word of mouth, festivals, who your family
is, how safe the vans are). The website's job is not to *replace* that, it is to *reflect*
it so an online visitor recognises the school they have already heard about.

## The reputation engine in Kullu (how trust actually forms here)
1. **Word of mouth and family standing.** Parents ask neighbours, relatives, their own
   elders. A 16-year-old school run by a known local family, with siblings and now second
   children enrolled, is trusted before a single ad is seen. → The site should foreground
   *longevity, family, and continuity of care*, not slogans.
2. **Safety, especially transport.** The single biggest anxiety for hill-town parents is
   children on mountain roads. Trained drivers, maintained vans, supervised routes, secure
   campus, trained staff, good food. This is not a footnote here, it is a headline.
3. **Sanskar and discipline.** Himachali families want children who stay grounded, respectful,
   and rooted, alongside good English and results. Values and character carry real weight.
4. **Rootedness in the valley.** Being *of* Kullu, not just *in* it. Kids Planet sits at
   **Dhalpur**, the ground where the deities of Kullu Dussehra camp for seven days. Taking
   part in Dussehra, Nati, Himachali Day, caring for the valley's environment, celebrating
   Pahari culture and language — this is what makes a school part of the community fabric.
5. **Aspiration without pretension.** Parents want their children to reach far ("Excel &
   Explore", "Aim for the Stars") while staying humble. Overclaiming reads as boastful and
   erodes trust in a close community.
6. **Approachable, accountable leadership.** In a small town, trust is personal. A Chairman
   and Principal who is present, known, and answerable matters more than any brochure.

## What works / what does not (translated for the site)
- **Works:** real photos of real school life and festivals; safety made concrete; the family
  story; teacher stability; environment and outdoors; character and belonging (the houses);
  a warm, humble, confident voice; the Dhalpur/valley connection.
- **Does not work:** boastful claims, board-exam framing, leaning on the affiliation as the
  selling point, generic stock, repeating one line ("known by name") until it rings hollow.

## Specific corrections Karan asked for
- **Drop the "first words to board year" claim.** The school runs to Class 8, and there is no
  board year here. Reframe the journey positively as *the foundation years* — the years that
  shape a child — and as sending confident, curious young people on well prepared for what
  comes next. Do not draw attention to the Class 8 ceiling.
- **De-emphasise HPBOSE.** Keep affiliation factually available (footer / fine print, UDISE)
  but do not lead with it or repeat it. The school's pull is its people, values, safety, and
  rootedness, not its board. **Do not mention ICSE anywhere** (a future direction, not public).

## New / changed sections for the site
- **"Safe hands" section** — concrete: vans and trained drivers on valley roads, supervised
  campus and gates, staff trained in first aid, nutritious meals, known pickup protocols.
- **"Rooted in the valley" section** — Dhalpur home, Kullu Dussehra and Nati, Himachali Day,
  care for the mountains and environment, Pahari culture kept alive. Real event/excursion
  photos. This is the offline reputation, made visible.
- **Reframe "How we learn"** around foundation years, character, confidence, curiosity, and
  well-rounded growth (arts, sport, the outdoors), not boards or affiliation.
- **Longevity and family** — 16 years, a local family, generations of the valley's children.
- Keep the voice humble and warm. Aspiration is a light touch ("Aim for the Stars" in two
  places only), grounded by everything above.
